Most people who work in tech feel behind on the news, and most of the fixes make it worse: more newsletters, more feeds, more tabs. Here is a routine that takes five minutes on a normal day and fifteen on a big one.

## Morning: five minutes, one place

Read one brief, not ten sources. Skim the headlines first, then read the two or three stories that touch your work. Ignore the rest without guilt: if a story matters, it will still matter tomorrow, and the brief will show it again.

**With ReadKernel:** open [today's brief](/today) or your morning email. If you only care about some topics, [build your own brief](/brief/customize) with fewer stories.

## When something is big: go one level deeper

For the one story that actually affects you, open the original coverage. Read the company's own announcement first, then one independent outlet. That's usually enough to know what happened and what people think about it.

**With ReadKernel:** every story page lists all its sources, and ongoing stories show how they developed day by day.

## Weekly: catch what you skipped

Once a week, scan the week's top stories. It catches the things you skipped on busy days, and it shows which stories kept going (the ones that really mattered) and which faded.

**With ReadKernel:** switch to the weekly email if daily is too much, or browse the [archive](/brief).

## What to stop doing

- **Doom-scrolling social feeds for news.** They reward speed and anger, not accuracy.
- **Subscribing to everything.** Five newsletters you don't open are worse than one you do.
- **Reading every take.** One good analysis beats ten hot takes.

Keeping up is not about reading more. It's about reading the right five minutes.
